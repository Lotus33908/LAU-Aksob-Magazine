Where the region's finance goes next

By the Ledger editorial team · 5 min read · tap the tags
Feature image

For decades, banking in the Levant meant branches, relationships and paper. That model is being rewritten. Mobile wallets, instant payments and rules are changing how money moves, and the skills a finance graduate needs are changing with them.

Three forces stand out. Digital infrastructure: payment rails that once took days now settle in seconds, and regulators are publishing frameworks for licensed firms. Sustainability: lenders increasingly price risk into decisions, so analysts must read a sustainability report as fluently as a balance sheet. And talent: the most valuable graduates pair quantitative depth with the judgement to ask whether a model answers the right question.

At AKSOB these themes shape the classroom: valuation models on live data, cases drawn from regional firms, and presentations to the practitioners who made the decisions being studied.

Quick questionHow long does a real-time payment take to settle?Hold the circle until it pops
Seconds.Real-time rails settle in under ten seconds, around the clock, even on holidays. Banks that still batch payments overnight are learning to keep up.

In short

  1. Payments are becoming instant and programmable.
  2. ESG literacy is now a core analyst skill.
  3. Judgement beats tooling. Ask the right question.

Feature · Three trends05

Three trends to watch

Ride-hailing apps that lend to drivers, retailers that offer instalments at checkout: financial products are moving to where customers already are. For graduates, product thinking now matters as much as credit analysis.

Debt raised specifically for environmental projects has grown from a niche into a mainstream asset class. Issuers must report impact, which creates demand for analysts who can verify it.

Banks now scan transactions, documents and headlines in real time to spot fraud and credit deterioration. The edge belongs to teams who understand both the statistics and the business.
